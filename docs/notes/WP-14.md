# WP-14 proof: the fidelity guard and the `Cleaner` port

The proof step of WP-14, 2026-11-19, on the build Mac: macOS 27.3 (26C5047e), Xcode 27.2, the on-device model "AFM 3 Core Advanced". Its code is on the branch `wp/14-proof`, where PR 1 starts. This note has two readers: Paolo, who chooses the cleanup default at the G4 sitting, and WP-02, which writes the `Cleaner` port.

Every example here is synthetic. The run on the owner's ten spontaneous notes is still to come: their recordings and transcripts are not on the Mac yet (see [The owner's notes](#the-owners-notes)).

## For Paolo: what the guard lets through

Knot can show a voice note three ways:

- **Off**: the raw transcript, as the transcriber wrote it.
- **Punctuation only**: rows 1 to 4 below. No word is removed.
- **Full cleanup**: rows 1 to 8.

Whichever you choose, the guard compares the model's answer with the raw transcript, word by word. When the answer differs in a way the table does not allow, the guard refuses it and the note shows the raw transcript. A refusal loses nothing. The note is just less tidy.

| # | Difference | Setting | The guard lets this through | The guard refuses this |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Letter case | Both | "send the notes to anna on friday" → "Send the notes to Anna on Friday" | "ask dr okafor" → "Ask Doctor Okafor": a word changed, not only its case |
| 2 | Punctuation and hyphens between letters | Both | "don't forget forty two chairs for the 3rd" → "Don't forget forty-two chairs for the 3rd!" | "pay 240 dollars and 50 cents" → "Pay $240.50.", and "meet at ten thirty" → "Meet at 10:30.": numbers stay as you said them. An apostrophe, a minus sign and a leading point belong to their word: "well go" → "We'll go", "-5 degrees" → "5 degrees", ".5" → "5" |
| 3 | Paragraph and line breaks | Both | "the hose is leaking separate thing the ladder is on loan" → "The hose is leaking." and a new paragraph, "Separate thing: the ladder is on loan." | "call mum then book the car" → "Book the car." then "Call mum then.": the order changed |
| 4 | List markers at the start of a line: "- ", or "1." and "1)" counting from 1 by one, with a space after | Both | "shopping list eggs milk bread" → "Shopping list:" then "- eggs", "- milk", "- bread", or "1. eggs", "2. milk", "3. bread" | A number you said never becomes a marker: "2 eggs" → "2. eggs", "buy 1 egg 2 lemons" → "1. egg", "2. lemons", and "packing first the passport second the charger" → "1. the passport", "2. the charger". Also "buy eggs bread" → "12. eggs", "2. bread": a marker that does not count from 1. Only a number you ended a sentence with may start a line: "we need chairs. 10. no 12." → a paragraph "10. No, 12." |
| 5 | Filler sounds: um, uh, er, erm, ah, eh, hm, hmm, mm, mhm | Full | "um call the uh bank" → "Call the bank." | "you know the answer so just decide" → "The answer, so just decide.": "you know", "like", "I mean", "so" and "well" are words, not fillers |
| 6 | A word or words said twice, but never a negation, a number, a date word or a name | Full | "the the meeting went well" → "The meeting went well." | "the the meeting went well" → "Meeting went well.": both copies gone. Also "it's not not true" → "It's not true.", "the code is 4 4 1 9" → "4 1 9", "twenty twenty two" → "twenty-two": a repeat of those may be meant |
| 7 | A restart: at most four words, after which you begin again with the same two first words. The brief says one word; the guard asks for two, which the orchestrator accepted, because one word lets far more through (below) | Full | "can you pick up can you grab the parcel from the post office" → "Can you grab the parcel from the post office?" | "meet on Tuesday no Wednesday" → "Meet on Wednesday.": a change of mind stays as you said it. Also "get eggs get milk" → "Get milk.": only one word comes again, so it may be a list. Also "the code is 4 7 1 9 sorry 4 7 9 1" → "The code is 4 7 9 1." |
| 8 | How much may go | Full | Up to 35% of the words that are not filler sounds | "can you can you can you hear me" → "Can you hear me?": half the words gone. PR 1 set 35% from the evaluation set, whose most is c27's 31% |

In a restart, a negation, a number, a name or a date word may go only when the same word comes back at the same place straight after, as "not" does in "it's not it's not the blue folder" → "It's not the blue folder.", and one for one: a word removed twice must come back twice.

**What the guard refuses although it is right.** A refusal keeps the raw text, so these cost tidiness, not meaning: "I can not come" → "I cannot come.", "tell Anna tell Anna's mum" → "Tell Anna's mum.", and "packing 1 the passport 2 the charger" → a numbered list "1. the passport", "2. the charger", since no rule tells a spoken count from a numbering. Also a numbered list that a long note's second piece numbers on ("5.", "6." …): each piece is checked on its own, where "5." counts on from nothing.

**What the guard cannot see.** It checks words, not meaning, so these always get through. "Show original" is always one action away.

- Punctuation that changes the meaning: "no more than five people can come" → "No. More than five people can come.", or "invite Anna Maria and Joe" → "Invite Anna, Maria and Joe."
- A question mark on a statement. The model did this in a synthetic run: "remind me that I owe Tomasz 45 pounds for the concert tickets?"
- Case that changes a word: "tell us about it" → "Tell US about it."
- A restart that was really two things, or a correction of one word: the guard cannot tell a correction from a list. "buy the eggs buy the milk" → "Buy the milk." loses the eggs, and "we call her anna we call her joe at home" → "We call her Joe at home." loses a name, because a name the transcriber wrote in lower case is not known as a name. Also "call the bank call the school about the loan for the car" → "Call the school about the loan for the car."
- A protected word that a restart removed is counted one for one over the whole note, not over the restart: "we will not go we will not go we will not stay home …" may lose two "not"s when a third is said later in the note ("… we do not know when"). Each removed "not" still comes again at its place in the start that follows.
- A number you ended a sentence with may start a list line: "is it 2? eggs" may become "Is it" and a line "2. eggs", since "?" ends a sentence.
- A meant repeat of any other word: "he had had enough" → "He had enough.", "I like like him" → "I like him." The guard removes a repeat of a word that is not a negation, a number, a date word or a name. A capital is a name's only mark, so "Bora Bora" is kept and "bora bora" is not.
- A title. KnotCleanup drops a title whose words all stand in one sentence that holds a negation the title leaves out ("I'm not sure we should cancel the booking." named "cancel the booking"), but a title can still miss what a note means.

**What the synthetic runs showed.** These are the 30 cases of `Fixtures/cleanup/cases.json`, run four times with identical results.

- Full cleanup: the guard accepted 26 answers and refused 4. Each refusal was right. Two answers dropped an "and", one dropped "like", and one turned "um" into "...". After the guard, all 30 cases keep their meaning, the 17 meaning-critical ones included.
- Before the guard, the model changed the meaning of 2 of the 30 answers. In both it dropped an "and", and the guard caught both.
- Punctuation only: the guard accepted 27 and refused 3. The model removed or changed a word although its instructions told it not to.

**What PR 1 measured.** The same 30 cases through the cleaner the app uses, `ModelCleaner`, on the same model and build, in `Fixtures/results/cleanup/synthetic.json`:

- Full cleanup: the guard accepted 28 answers and refused 2, each rightly: c14's answer dropped an "and", which changed its meaning, and c29's dropped "like". All 17 meaning-critical cases keep their meaning after the guard.
- Punctuation only: the guard accepted 27 and refused 3, where the model removed a word (c18's repeated "the", c20's "and") or added one (c22, a stray tag).
- Two runs gave the same file byte for byte.

**What the review of PR 1 changed, and measured.** The review found changes of meaning that the guard accepted. Most are now refused (rows 2, 4 and 6). What the guard cannot see is above, and the evaluation now measures it: each meaning-critical case states where its cleanup may put a stop, a pause or a question (its readings), so that a break in a place nobody foresaw fails. Eleven cases were added and counted apart: five of the kinds the guard cannot see, and six written as the transcriber writes. Of the transcriber's nine synthetic transcripts in `Fixtures/results/speech/synthetic.json`, one is empty (silence); seven start with a capital, eight hold punctuation, numbers are written 29 times in digits (each run of digits, such as "3rd", "$240.50" or "1204") and 15 times in words, and four fillers are left in. On the same model and build, 41 cases:

- Full cleanup: the guard accepted 39 answers and refused 2: c14 and c28 each dropped an "and".
- **Four changed meanings reach the note**, and the guard cannot see any of them. Punctuation alone changed three: c15 "Meet at ten thirty two. People from Finance are joining." (the time changed and the count of people is gone), c30 "… so just decide?" and x03 "Call the bank, call the school, about the loan?" (both made questions). One word went that the guard allows: x05 "I think I like like him" → "I think I like him."
- Of the 17 meaning-critical cases of the first set, 15 pass after the guard. The six in the transcriber's form all pass.
- Punctuation only: the guard accepted 35 and refused 6; five changed meanings reach the note, all by punctuation and all questions on statements: c12, c15, c28, c30 and x03. Every case is judged for a question added at its end; c17 may fairly end as one, and says so.
- Titles: 28 of 41 kept, 6 dropped for a negation left out. Two of those were fair titles: "Don't sign yet" for t01 and "Flight next Friday" for t06, dropped because the next sentence holds a "not" ("Not until they fix the heating.", "Not Thursday."). That is the price of reading the next sentence for a take-back.
- A sentence on repeats in the instructions, which the review first asked for, cost c11 its comma ("Buy 2 no 3") in every wording tried, so it is not there: the guard keeps the rule whatever the model is told.

**One choice for you.** The guard does not treat "like", "you know" and "I mean" as fillers, because they often carry meaning ("you know the answer"). Two of WP-04's 30 reference cleanups remove them (c17, c29). The guard refuses those, and the note keeps the raw text. That is the safe default. Teaching the guard when these words are only fillers is a later feature, like the rest of what TODOS.md defers.

## For WP-02: does the draft `Cleaner` port fit?

The draft is docs/architecture.md §5: "Report availability. Turn a raw transcript into a title and body, or decline." Its shape fits. Five things need to be exact.

**1. Availability has three states, and it changes.** `SystemLanguageModel.availability` is `.available` or `.unavailable(.deviceNotEligible | .appleIntelligenceNotEnabled | .modelNotReady)`, and the class is `Observable`. Add `supportsLocale(_:)` for English. The port should report three states:

- available;
- not ready, which is a deferral;
- unavailable with a reason, after which the raw transcript is final.

It should also publish changes, because §4 runs deferred jobs again when the model's availability changes.

**2. No streaming.** The port should be one async call that returns the whole result:

- The guard needs the whole answer. `streamResponse` yields cumulative snapshots of partial content, and only the last one can be checked.
- A cancelled stream that is iterated with `for try await` **ends without an error**. In the probe it ended after 0 or 1 snapshots. A caller could take that partial answer for a finished one.
- `collect()` on the same stream throws `CancellationError`. `respond` does too.

The implementation uses `respond` and never iterates a stream.

**3. Cancellation is the task's.** There is no cancel method. Cancelling the task made `respond` throw `CancellationError` within 70 ms. Afterwards the session's transcript was as before the request, and the session served the next request. The port treats cancellation as a deferral (the job yields). It is never an attempt and never a decline.

**4. Errors.** In the OS 27 SDK `LanguageModelError` replaces the deprecated `LanguageModelSession.GenerationError`. `LanguageModelError` is `@nonexhaustive`, so the mapping needs a default. The mapping below is what the proof's `ModelCleanerFailure(_:)` does, and a test covers each row:

| Error | Class | Port case |
|---|---|---|
| `CancellationError` | Deferral | cancelled (the job yields) |
| `LanguageModelError.rateLimited` | Deferral | rate limited, with its `resetDate` as the job's `notBefore` |
| `SystemLanguageModel.Error.assetsUnavailable` | Deferral | model not ready |
| `LanguageModelError.refusal` | Decline | refusal |
| `LanguageModelError.guardrailViolation` | Decline | guardrail, **missing from the taxonomy** |
| `GeneratedContent.ParsingError` | Decline | malformed (only with guided generation) |
| An empty answer | Decline | empty |
| The guard refuses | Decline | guard rejected, with the guard's code |
| `LanguageModelError.contextSizeExceeded` | Decline | too long. Budgeting prevents it, so it should never happen |
| `LanguageModelError.unsupportedLanguageOrLocale` | Decline | language |
| `LanguageModelError.unsupportedCapability`, `.unsupportedGenerationGuide`, `.unsupportedTranscriptContent` | Decline | unsupported |
| `LanguageModelError.timeout` | Attempt | timed out |
| `LanguageModelSession.Error.concurrentRequests` | Attempt | busy. A session takes one request at a time: the second of two concurrent requests threw this. Use one session per request |
| Anything else | Attempt | unknown |

A decline is a result, not a thrown error. The job ends `ready`, with the raw transcript as its text, which is the finished state of §4. Deferrals and attempts are thrown.

**Logging.** The errors of Foundation Models carry note text. A `ParsingError`'s `rawContent` and `debugDescription` hold the generated text: the probe printed it. A refusal has an `explanation`. Log the case only, never `debugDescription`, `localizedDescription` or `explanation`.

**5. Title and body are two requests, not one guided generation.** On 9 harmless synthetic transcripts with trigger words ("kill the old process", "cut my finger … blood everywhere"), guided generation of `{ title, body }` was refused as a guardrail violation 2 times. That happened with the default guardrails and with `permissiveContentTransformations`. Plain `String` generation with `permissiveContentTransformations` cleaned all 9. So the body is plain text, and the title is a second request whose failure only drops the title. The proof's titles then pass or fail the title check of §9. In the probe, one title used a word that was not in the note ("Dentist Appointment"), and the check dropped it.

**The proposed port**, for WP-02 to settle:

```swift
public protocol Cleaner: Sendable {
    func availability() async -> CleanupAvailability
    func availabilityChanges() -> AsyncStream<CleanupAvailability>
    /// Cleans one raw transcript. Cancelling the calling task cancels the request.
    func clean(_ rawTranscript: String, proposeTitle: Bool) async throws(KnotError) -> CleanupResult
}

public enum CleanupAvailability: Sendable, Equatable {
    case available
    case notReady                                  // a deferral
    case unavailable(CleanupUnavailableReason)     // deviceNotEligible, appleIntelligenceOff, language
}

public struct CleanupResult: Sendable, Equatable {
    public enum Outcome: Sendable, Equatable {
        case cleaned(body: String)
        case declined(CleanupDecline)              // the raw transcript stays the text
    }
    public let outcome: Outcome
    public let title: String?                      // nil when none passes KnotCore's title check
    public let osBuild: String                     // the build whose model ran
    public let modelName: String                   // SystemLanguageModel.variant.displayName
}
```

`clean` throws only deferrals: model not ready, rate limited (with a retry date) and cancelled. It also throws attempts (timed out, busy, unknown) and model unavailable, which the queue treats as final. The fake that WP-02 ships must script every outcome, each deferral, a cancellation during a request, and availability changing from not ready to available. The contract suite runs those cases against the fake and against the real cleaner on the Mac.

**Budgeting**, for PR 1:

- `contextSize` is 8192 on this Mac. `tokenCount(for:)` counts a `String`, `Instructions` and a `GenerationSchema`, and each response reports its input and output tokens in `usage`.
- The answer is about as long as the transcript. So one request holds about (8192 − instructions − 64) / 2, roughly 3,900 tokens of transcript: about 3,500 words, or 20 minutes of speech. A synthetic ten-minute text of 1,702 words was cleaned in one request.
- Chunking on sentence boundaries starts at about 20 minutes. PR 1 proves "a 10-minute transcript is cleaned in chunks with nothing dropped" with a lowered budget. The chunker's tests do that already with a word-count counter.
- The proof caps each answer at twice its piece's tokens plus 64, so a runaway answer stops and the guard refuses it.
- Speed: about 58 output tokens a second, so 840 words took 15 s. That is a measurement, not a target.

**Determinism.** `GenerationOptions(samplingMode: .greedy)` gave the same 30 answers in four runs, in a fresh session for each request. The last two results files were identical byte for byte. The model also names itself: `SystemLanguageModel.variant.displayName`. Recording it beside the OS build costs nothing.

## Changes this asks of the architecture

1. **§9, cleanup.** Replace "a `LanguageModelSession` on `SystemLanguageModel.default`, with guided generation of `{ title, body }`" with: plain-text generation of the body on `SystemLanguageModel(useCase: .general, guardrails: .permissiveContentTransformations)`, a separate request for the title, and a new session for each request.
2. **§9, the guard's restart rule.** "A short restart whose first word comes again straight after" becomes: at most four words, whose first **two** words come again straight after. A protected word survives only at the **same place** in the restart. Without this, "get eggs get milk" → "Get milk." and "4 7 1 9 sorry 4 7 9 1" → "4 7 9 1" both pass.
3. **§9, the closed list of fillers** holds sounds only: um, uh, er, erm, ah, eh, hm, hmm, mm, mhm. "Like", "you know" and "I mean" are not on it (case c30). The notes of WP-04's c17 and c29 in `Fixtures/cleanup/cases.json` say those words go. With this list, the guard refuses those two references, which is safe, and the orchestrator may want to reword their notes.
4. **§9, the share limit** counts only removed words that are not filler sounds. Otherwise "um uh yes" would be refused. Provisionally it is 40%, and PR 1 sets it.
5. **§5, the error taxonomy.** "Cleanup declined" gains guardrail violation, too long, language and unsupported. The deferrals gain a cancellation (yield) and a rate limit that carries a retry date. The attempts gain a cleanup timeout.
6. **§5, the `Cleaner` port.** Availability has three states and publishes changes. A decline is a result. The port does not stream.
7. **§20, new platform facts.** Each comes from a probe on this Mac on 2026-11-19:
   - `contextSize` is 8192 with "AFM 3 Core Advanced" on macOS 27.3 (26C5047e).
   - A cancelled `ResponseStream` that is iterated ends without an error, and `collect()` throws.
   - A `LanguageModelSession` takes one request at a time.
   - Guided generation trips guardrails that plain `String` generation with `permissiveContentTransformations` does not.
   - The errors' descriptions can hold generated text.
   - `LanguageModelError` (OS 27) replaces `GenerationError`.
