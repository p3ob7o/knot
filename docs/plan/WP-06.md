## Build

- The soak test: 500 captures on the Mac from a fixed seed, mixing typed text, fixture recordings of at most two seconds and fixture drawings, with the scripted transcriber, cleaner and recogniser and an injected clock, finishing inside 3 minutes.
- WP-06 covers typed captures first. WP-11, WP-12 and WP-15 each add one file of kill points in their own PRs, and the soak is among their merge criteria.
- The harness is a child process over the real store, killed at points the seed chooses from a named list, then relaunched. The child is the executable that WP-01 PR 1 declares.
- The harness follows `docs/architecture.md` §17: the parent process is the witness, and one shared consistency check ends the run.
- It asserts three things: no capture row is still open, every segment that originated on the device has a row, and every stored segment holds at least the revision the engine reported as saved.
- The checks, as the orchestrator ruled them on the review of pull request 70:
  - The parent reads what the stream returns, not only the store's rows.
  - It checks every piece of a long capture.
  - It checks the script's text exactly.
  - It checks after each launch's recovery, not only at the end of the run.
  - A run that its time box cut short is not green.
- It runs under `scripts/verify.sh --soak`, at each milestone and in the merge gate of any PR that touches one of the paths `docs/architecture.md` §17 names: KnotCore, KnotStore, KnotCapture, KnotAudio, KnotApp, the harness or the pinned GRDB version.
- `scripts/verify.sh` takes `--seed`, and `--gate` adds the soak from the diff.
- The soak tier also takes the contract check `everyOrderKeepsTheEdit` on the real store. It runs 20,160 orders, about seven minutes, so WP-10 left it out of `--quick`.
- The watch half is not in this harness. WP-17 proves it with the fake transport: every manifest is still on the watch or acknowledged.

### WP-06a

- The soak's step in `scripts/verify.sh`, split out of the first PR so that the harness merges without the Mac UI step. It is on `main` since pull request 79.
- The step runs every test of the `KnotSoak` target with `swift test --filter`, under the test runs' time limit, with the seed in `KNOT_SOAK_SEED`. The seed comes from `--seed`, or a fresh one is drawn, and the step prints it first.
- A run in which no test ran, or in which a test was skipped or cancelled, fails.
- A pass prints the soak's own summary lines, `capture ledger soak:` and `everyOrderKeepsTheEdit:`. A failure prints the verdict's lines, the seed, the kill points and where the store was kept, and the command that repeats the run with that seed. It is never retried.
- The gate's `soak` line in `scripts/gate-steps.txt` is required: a gate whose diff reaches the paths of `SOAK` in `scripts/lib/knot_tool.py` runs the step with a fresh seed.

### WP-06b

- A small pull request beside the two this package planned, by the orchestrator's ruling of 2026-11-22. The soak asked that every point of its list kill at least once in a run, and a run at a fresh seed was red when one did not: measured on main, the ledger soak at seed 7301 was red twice in two runs for a point armed nine times that its launches never brought about, where six fresh seeds were green, several by one kill.
- A soak at a fresh seed is never red because a point did not kill: a seed whose script does not bring a point about is no fault of the code under test. It still prints each point's kills and armings, and its draws still favour the points that have not killed.
- That every point of a list can kill is held by a test of its own, `KillPointCoverage`, on a fixed seed: one run for each point, armed at its first pass in one launch in two, and red when the point has not killed by the script's end. A point that matches nothing, and one the child never reaches, are red there. Any list goes through it, WP-11's, WP-12's and WP-15's too.
- The child passes the step `during_recording` also when its wait for a recording's frames ends, so that every recording with a durable frame passes it at least once.

### WP-06c

- A small pull request beside those this package planned, by the orchestrator's ruling of 2026-11-23, for a fault of the soak's child and not of the code under test. The child wrote its last line, `done`, and then exited with the engine's work still in flight, and its log sink passes every error line on to the parent. A line could so follow `done`, and the witness, whose rule is that `done` is a launch's last line, read `launch_did_not_finish` with every capture run. Seen once in 110 runs at 06b; since 06b each soak at the gate runs some thirty covering runs with launches that quit by themselves, and on 2026-11-23 one of them failed a gate. The late line, found by running that launch a hundred times: an item of the launch's last capture, still being ingested at the exit, kept for the next launch on a busy store.
- Every line of the child goes through one lock, and the last line closes the writer under it: a line that comes after is refused and counted, never written. The witness's rule is unchanged.
- A fault in which the engine's log writes an error after the last line, with two tests that rest on no chance: the line does not reach the parent, and a soak whose every launch logs after `done` is green.

### WP-06d

- A small pull request beside those this package planned, by the orchestrator's ruling of 2026-11-22, made with WP-15's first pull request. Until it the soak's child built a processing queue and never drained it, so no job ran in the soak, and the queue's deaths were held by the package's tests alone.
- The child drains the queue after recovery in every launch, awaited before its last line, on scripted speech and cleanup of its own. A recording's phrases are a function of its capture ID, read in runs of two seconds so that a transcription takes more than one run, and the cleanup is not idempotent. No model is used and no hardware input is opened. The queue is given the store's device, as the live composition gives it.
- The witness holds jobs to what they leave after the last launch. A stored recording has one job of each kind, and no other segment has a job. Every job is done, or failed with `job_died_twice` after exactly two deaths, with its segment in its failed step: `JobMachine.maximumDeaths` is 2, and nothing runs such a job again without a retry, which the child never asks. A job has one death for each number of deaths a launch found it running with: a kill between the child's line and the queue's save shows the same death twice, and it counts once. A stored recording whose segment the store does not return is a problem of its own. The transcript is the script's phrases, each once, or a prefix of them for a transcription that died twice. The text is the scripted cleanup of that transcript, applied once. Texts are compared by capture ID, and a problem names one by its length and hash only.
- Each check is shown red by an injected fault of its own, after a green control, but one: a stored recording with no segment, which the store cannot be made to hold.
- WP-15's file of kill points gains the queue's five log events, `transcript_run_saved`, `transcribed`, `clean_job_written`, `cleanup_applied` and `job_found_running`, and each kills in its covering run. docs/plan/WP-15.md's criterion on that file is met by the two pull requests together: ingest's points with WP-15 PR 1, processing's with this row.
- A known limit: the drain runs before a launch's captures, or after them in a launch that recovers while it captures. So no job runs while a capture is open, and no kill leaves both an open capture and a running job.

### WP-06e

- A small pull request beside those this package planned, by the orchestrator's ruling of 2026-11-24. Test code only: the soak's time box.
- What was red. The hosted nightly's package tests under Thread Sanitizer, on 2026-11-24: seven runs of the soak's own tests were cut short by their time box of 180 s, at 244 to 254 s, the controls of row 06d's fault tests among them, and a run its box cuts short is not green. No sanitizer report that night.
- The cause is load, and nothing waits that should not. On the hosted runner the bundle's 41 tests start their sanitized children together, and a run of 30 captures needs about 350 s there. The same runs took 24 to 34 s under the sanitizer on the build Mac that night.
- The cure. The box is 720 s when the test process runs under the sanitizer, which it tells by the sanitizer's runtime being loaded, and 180 s otherwise; the configuration line carries it. Fewer captures would have changed what the fault tests and the covering runs show; a box changes nothing of what a run does. A run cut short stays not green.
- How it was shown. A hosted run of the nightly started by hand on the pull request's branch, on a commit that also holds the cure of the recorder's false sanitizer report: its sanitizer step green in 24m26s. On the build Mac: 41 tests under the sanitizer in 115.7 s with `box=720`, and plain in 91.3 s with `box=180`.
- Known. A green hosted run keeps no log, so the soak's own times there are not known; keeping the step's log on a green run is the scripts' or the workflow's. The 45 to 91 s first given here for the gate's soak runs were wrong: those 50 runs were filtered to three suites with `KNOT_SOAK_FILTER`, with far less beside them than the gate's whole step. What the whole step takes is under WP-06f.

### WP-06f

- A small pull request beside those this package planned, by the orchestrator's ruling of 2026-11-24. Test code only: the soak's time box.
- What was seen. Every soak run had one box of 180 s, the tests' runs of 30 captures and the tier's runs of 500 alike. Since row 06d a run of 500 captures takes 136 to 159 s with the whole soak step on a quiet build Mac. At the gate of pull request 115, with the owner at the Mac, the two took 177.3 and 179.5 s. Beside two builds one was cut at 184 s with 386 of 500 captures made. A run its box cuts short is not green, so a gate could go red with no fault in the code.
- The rule. A run's box is 60 s, one second for each typed capture and three for each recording, never less than 180 s, and four times that under the sanitizer. A run of 500 captures gets about 750 s, a run of 200 about 340 s, and the tests' own runs keep 180 s. A replay keeps the box of the run it repeats. A test holds the rule, and a run cut short stays not green.
- How it was shown. The whole soak step on the pull request's head: green, 42 tests in 9m49s, the runs of 500 captures in 150.5 and 151.1 s against a box of 778 s.
- Known. The hosted quick check runs none of the tier's long runs: they run under the soak tier and at the gate's soak step.

## Merge criteria

- `scripts/verify.sh --quick` passes.
- The soak passes under `scripts/verify.sh --soak`, inside its 3 minutes, with its three assertions holding.
- WP-06 merges only when four injected faults each turn the soak red: an acknowledgement before the write, a skipped capture row, a dropped last checkpoint and a capture stored twice.
- Package tests use Swift Testing and UI tests use XCTest, as docs/architecture.md §17 states.

## Failure rules

- When the soak fails it prints its seed, its kill point and where the store was kept. A soak failure is never retried to green.
- The checks are shown to fail: the four injected faults must each turn the soak red before WP-06 merges.
- The soak is built by WP-06 and not by WP-22. WP-22 PR 2 keeps the end-to-end suite and no longer builds the soak.
- WP-06 also gets a second opinion from a different model. Every finding the orchestrator sets aside is written in the PR and in the journal.
- The soak's child uses the fakes, whose code compiles only in a debug build, or in a build that sets `KNOT_FAKES`. An optimised build of the child sets `KNOT_FAKES`.
