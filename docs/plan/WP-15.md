## Build

### PR 1

- The durable job queue: transcribe, clean and title, recognise; retry with backoff; resume at launch.
- Job states and counts. Jobs have four named states. Jobs count attempts, deferrals and deaths apart, as docs/architecture.md §4 states, and a job fails after five attempts. The error taxonomy gains the deferral class and three cases: text too long, a capture stopped because the disk filled, and a cursor from another store generation. WP-02 and WP-15 carry it.
- An interrupted transcription continues from its last saved range.
- The queue passes a budget to every transcription run: a run holds its window's frames in memory, about 11.5 MB for 60 s at 48 kHz, and a run of thirty minutes with no budget about 345 MB (docs/architecture.md §9). Only the tests ask for a run with no budget.
- A run with no pieces that moves on is applied like any run: the store moves the job's resume point and adds no text. Silence never makes a run read more than its budget.
- The queue wakes on the transcriber's readiness changes (`KnotSpeechLive.readinessChanges(_:)`), so that a deferred job runs when the model's availability changes.
- The store writes the transcribe job of every recording it stores, `JobID.transcribe(of:)`, in the transaction that stores it. A run's text and the job's resume time are saved together, on that job, and a job's saved state only moves forward: a save names the job's revision it read (docs/architecture.md §4 and §5).
- A recording with no recognised words is ready with empty text.
- Every voice segment records the engine, the locale and the OS build that produced its transcript, as `transcription` on `Segment` in docs/architecture.md §4. Each cleaned segment records the OS build whose model made it. The queue stores both with the result of a job.
- Machine text goes through the store. Replacing text checks the precedence of edited, cleaned and raw inside the store, so the queue carries no check of its own.
- Titles. The clean job proposes a title only for the note that holds its recording when the job finishes, and only if that recording is the note's first segment and the note has none. The check that a generated title uses only words from its note is one function in KnotCore.
- Who processes a recording, as docs/architecture.md §11 fixes it. `processingOwner` on `Segment` says which device runs a segment's jobs, and ownership of a recording carries a generation. The queue runs the jobs this device owns. WP-16 owns the rule on who processes a recording, with the tie-break by device ID, and its test. WP-10 PR 2 queues what a merge makes, through the store's interface.
- The inbox ingestor: idempotent by capture ID. It takes the items it finds together in the order their captures started, so a batch from the Watch that arrived out of order is placed as it was recorded (rule 7 of docs/product-spec.md §3).
- Late arrivals are placed by the store, not by WP-15. Ingest places a capture where its manifest says: the manifest of a capture that fell back to `Inbox/` records the placement that was asked for and the note the surface showed.
- The ingestor reads `purged` in what `ingest` and `recordKeptItem` answer: for a kept item whose failed segment was purged the store writes nothing, and the ingestor removes the item's files, its payloads first and its manifest last. A row that failed for another reason is not gone, and its capture, arriving again whole, is stored (docs/architecture.md §6, rule 2).
- Ingest goes through `NoteStore`'s one ingest operation, which reconciles an item with the capture's row in any state. Only the content revision it reports as stored lets the ingestor remove the file. A malformed item is written as a failed segment, and the call fails with `inbox_item_unreadable`, so the file stays. Only a delivery from outside the app process is late, and in v1 that is the Watch's: an item the app itself wrote is never late (docs/architecture.md §5 and §6).
- At launch ingest runs before recovery ends the open rows of earlier launches. The two orders give different results (docs/architecture.md §5).
- Manifests. `CaptureManifest` in KnotCore is the one versioned format for a recording in progress, an inbox item and a watch outbox file. Manifests carry a version, and WP-15 bounds a manifest.
- A large inbox is a bulk writer, and bulk writers commit in small batches.
- The files of an unreadable item go when its failed segment is deleted and then purged, and no later version stores that item (docs/architecture.md §6, rule 2).
- Launch recovery removes the temporary files that a cut write left in `Inbox/`, which are no items. `ItemLock` orders the engine and ingest in one process only, extensions write into `Inbox/`, and `DurableFile.removePartials` removes every partial it sees, so the rule must be safe against a writer in another process. The orchestrator settled it on 2026-11-22: launch recovery removes every partial in `Inbox/`, once a launch, in the app only, with `DurableFile.removePartials(in:)`; a writer in another process whose partial goes places the file again under a new name, once, since `DurableFile.place` runs again on `ENOENT`; and a second removal inside one place fails that write to its caller and leaves nothing half-written (docs/architecture.md §6, rule 2). This PR measures the retry and turns the soak's switch for those partials, `inboxPartialsAreRed`, to red.
- A kill point in the soak between an `Inbox/` item's two files.
- Pending and failed states published through `NoteStore`.
- Every log line about a capture carries its capture ID.

### PR 2

- Hooks for background execution on iOS, using the mechanism SP-1 chooses.
- The iOS runners of `BackgroundRunner`, the port that WP-02 PR 2 declares, in the shape that docs/notes/SP-1.md gives it: a runner passed in by whoever holds the time, scoped to one call, whose work reports progress. WP-05 writes the runner that runs work straight through (docs/architecture.md §5).
- SP-1 also answers whether cleanup with the on-device model finishes in the background after a recording stops.
- A step that finds no background time left is a deferral, as docs/architecture.md §4 states.
- A drain that runs on a `BackgroundRunner` skips recognise jobs. Recognition that finds the app not in the foreground is a deferral, and the job runs when the app comes forward (docs/architecture.md §4 and §10).

## Failure rules

- An unreadable inbox item becomes a failed segment that keeps its file. An item whose manifest cannot be read, or that a newer version wrote, is recorded through the store's `recordKeptItem`, named by the capture ID that its file names tell (docs/architecture.md §6, rule 2). The tracer's ingest does not call that operation: it leaves such an item in `Inbox/` and writes nothing for it.
- Manifests carry a version, and an item of a newer version than the build has its own named test below.
- After a third checksum mismatch from the watch, the damaged copy is ingested as failed and kept.
- A step that cannot run now is a deferral, not an attempt, as docs/architecture.md §4 states.
- A job found running at launch is a death, counted apart from attempts.
- Cleanup waits when the model is not ready.
- A soak failure is never retried to green. It prints its seed, its kill point and where the store was kept.
