# Open points of the fold

This file lists what the fold could not settle. While they rewrote the plan as `PLAN.md` and the briefs, the writers noted each place where the plan was silent, contradictory or out of date, and the auditor added notes of its own. Those notes are merged here into one numbered list, and each point was checked against the folded documents as they stand on 2026-11-19.

The file adds no requirement and changes no rule. It is for the orchestrator and the owner to work through before the packages concerned start. A point is closed by a decision recorded where it belongs: in a brief, in `PLAN.md`, in `docs/architecture.md` or in `docs/product-spec.md`. The point is then removed from this file.

A second-model review then read the fold item by item, and the points it settled are gone from the list: who owns `docs/plan/`, where `AppEnvironment` is first declared, which thread runs WP-02, who builds the Mac's About actions, what the MCP server does while access is off, and which PR embeds `knot`. The orchestrator has since settled three more: a crash host for KnotInk, who commits the evaluation results, and where a short note goes. The reviews of the first two packages settled who owns the crash hosts, the `knot-eval` target and `Package.resolved`. Before wave 1 the orchestrator settled eleven more: how a spike's report changes the architecture, the branch and the report of SP-5, the pull requests of WP-01 and WP-02 with the paths and the named tests the fold gave them, where the owner finds the guard's table, the two readings of the timed targets, the milestones of two measurements, the cleanup's OS build in §4, what §18 says of three scripts, and two rows of the owner's calendar. After the first code merged, the orchestrator settled four more: who sets `arrivedAt`, the reading of the named test that WP-05, WP-11 and WP-22 share, who records what made a transcript, and who owns the capture module's test folders. After the notes of SP-1, SP-2 and WP-18, the orchestrator settled five more: what SP-4's short note carries of CloudKit's record limit, what export does with a file that is on another device, that the PRs of WP-17, WP-19 and WP-34 stand as their briefs split them, who implements the seven supporting ports, which settles the half on WP-15 of the point on start rows, because the port of background time comes with WP-02's second PR, and how a drawing renders and exports before WP-18 merges. On 2026-11-20 the owner settled whether a speech or cleanup pull request waits for his recordings: it does not, and AGENTS.md says so.

Each section puts first the points that block the earliest packages. Item IDs such as `eng-071` are those of `docs/plan/amendments.md`.

## Rules that pull against each other

6. **The files of an unreadable inbox item.** The store writes an inbox item that cannot be read as a failed segment with no file, and answers `inbox_item_unreadable`. A repeat of the same item throws again and writes nothing (pull request 32, finding 71). docs/architecture.md §6, rule 2, says that such an item keeps the file, and that nothing in `Inbox/` is deleted before it is stored. WP-15's brief says that the failed segment "keeps its file". So the files stay in `Inbox/`, and every scan meets them again. No rule says how a person reaches the file from the failed segment, or what removes the files once the person has deleted that segment. The orchestrator settles it before WP-15's first PR.

7. **The first-run line on sync.** The empty stream on first run says that sync is on and where its switch is (docs/product-spec.md §12, `design-19`), and WP-21, WP-30 and WP-33 carry that line. ## Documents that lack what the plan says of them

27. **A test folder that no brief lists.** PLAN.md §7 says the paths in a brief include the package's tests (`dx-17`). WP-34 lists no test folder for the `knot` executable. The orchestrator settles it before WP-34 starts.

29. **The thresholds that left WP-10 and WP-30.** `eng-031` takes the time thresholds out of the merge criteria of WP-10 and WP-30, and WP-42's brief now names them: with 10,000 notes on the Mac, a stream page against 16 ms and a search against 50 ms, and the iPhone's launch against its warm target. docs/product-spec.md §14 has rows for the search and the launch and none for the stream page. 30. **Where an open drawing's saves go.** `CaptureEngine` publishes the revision of the content that is durably saved, and its comment says that a drawing's saves are not in the contract yet. `CaptureCheckpoint` carries text only, and an open drawing has no body in the store, so no operation of `NoteStore` makes the strokes of an open drawing durable. The ink types come with WP-02's third PR. It is settled there or with WP-18, before the first pull request that builds the canvas.

31. **A privacy manifest for a bare tool.** docs/architecture.md §19 asks for a required reason for each API that needs one, in every target. The `knot` tool is a bare executable: it has no `Resources` folder for a manifest, and it links GRDB statically, whose manifest sits in a bundle the tool does not carry. SP-3's spike app carried no manifest in its bare tool and did not try to embed one (docs/notes/SP-3.md). §19 does not say whether a bare executable carries a manifest of its own, or, if it cannot, whether its reasons go in the app's manifest. The upload of the distribution-signed check shows whether App Store Connect accepts the app's manifest for the tool. The orchestrator settles §19 with SP-3's report.

## For the owner

32. **A production schema for the rehearsal.** PLAN.md §5 has the move from CloudKit's development environment to production rehearsed between two development containers before M7. Deploying the CloudKit production schema needs Paolo's explicit authorisation. On 2026-11-20 he allowed SP-4 to deploy its schema to the production side of a throwaway container made only for the spike, and `docs/plan/SP-4.md` says so. His answer covers the spike alone. The plan does not say whether the rehearsal deploys anything to a production side (`ceo-23`, `eng-097`): as §5 words it, between two development containers, it does not. The point said that he decides before SP-4 starts. The spike no longer waits for it, so the orchestrator moved the decision: he decides when SP-4's report says what the rehearsal must show.

33. **Distribution signing in WP-43's dry run.** Distribution signing needs Paolo's word (`eng-074`). The plan does not say whether the dry run that is WP-43's merge criterion signs for distribution (`eng-033`). He decides before WP-43 starts.

34. **Which words are fillers.** WP-14's proof step treats only sounds as fillers: um, uh and the like. It leaves "like", "you know" and "I mean" as words, because they often carry meaning, so a cleanup that removes them is refused and the raw transcript stays (docs/notes/WP-14.md). He decides at the cleanup sitting of G4 whether they ever count as fillers.

35. **A segment whose save failed.** The table of product spec §5 says that "Needs you" means that a step failed, and it offers Retry. A typed capture or a drawing whose save failed is shown as "Needs you" too, with its content kept, and Knot stores it by itself when it can: nothing a person does retries it. So the state promises an action that this case does not have. He decides at the design round, G2, whether such a segment offers Retry or shows a state of its own. `docs/design/brief.md` already lists text that could not be saved among the states to design.
