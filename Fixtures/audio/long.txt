This is a long note about the weekend, the house and a few things for work, so that nothing gets lost while I walk. I will start with the house, because that is where most of the open questions are, and then move on to the garden, the trip in May and the work items that keep slipping from one week to the next.

The kitchen first. The builders say the old cabinets can come out on Monday, but only if the electrician has moved the sockets by then. The electrician can come on Friday morning, which leaves the weekend to empty the cupboards. Everything in the top cupboards can go into the boxes in the hall. The heavy pans should go to the shed, because they will not fit anywhere else and nobody needs them for a month.

The tiles are still undecided. The pale grey ones looked good in the shop, but in the sample box at home they look almost blue under the evening light. The warmer sand colour might be safer. The shop will lend us a larger panel of each if we ask before Wednesday, and that seems worth doing before we order forty square metres of something we only saw under shop lights.

For the floor, the plan is to keep the old boards in the hall and only replace the kitchen part. The carpenter thinks the boards under the sink are soft, which would explain the smell last winter. If they are rotten, he will cut out a section and fit new boards, and the cost goes up by roughly a fifth. He will know on the first day, so we should not decide anything about the floor finish until then.

Now the garden. The tomatoes need staking before the wind picks up again, and the netting over the strawberries has a tear on the side facing the fence. The hose by the shed is leaking at the tap, which probably only needs a new washer. The neighbours asked whether they could borrow the long ladder on Sunday, which is fine, as long as it comes back before the gutters are cleared.

The hedge along the lane has grown into the path again. Cutting it is a half day job with two people, and the green waste bins are already full until the next collection. It might be easier to book the council van for a single large pickup, which costs a small fee but saves three weekends of filling bins a little at a time.

The trip in May is mostly booked. The train leaves early on the Thursday, and the connection in the city is only twenty minutes, which is tight with luggage. There is a later connection that adds an hour but leaves more room for delays. The hotel can hold the room until late, so the later train is probably the calmer choice, and it means breakfast at home instead of at the station.

For the trip we still need a power adapter, a light rain jacket each, and the walking map for the coastal path. The guide book on the shelf is six years old, so some of the ferry times in it will be wrong. The tourist office has a page with the summer timetable, and it is worth printing it, because the phone signal on that coast was patchy last time.

Work next. The quarterly report is due at the end of the month, and the section on the support numbers is still missing. Those numbers come from the shared sheet, which someone reorganised last week, so the formulas in the summary tab may point at the wrong columns. Checking that is the first job on Monday, before anyone copies the totals into the slides.

The team meeting moved from Tuesday to Wednesday, at the same time. The agenda has three items: the new holiday calendar, the training budget, and who covers the front desk during the move. The holiday calendar is easy. The training budget needs a number from finance first, and the front desk question needs a short list of names, ideally people who have done it before.

There is also the question of the old laptops. Four of them are still in the cupboard, and they should be wiped and recycled rather than kept as spares, because nobody has used them in two years. The recycling company collects on the first Friday of each month, and the form needs serial numbers, which are printed on the bottom of each machine.

Back to home things. The car is due for its service next month, and the tyres were close to the limit at the last check. It makes sense to book both at the same garage, on the same morning, and to ask for a courtesy car. The garage closes for a week in the middle of the month, so the booking should go in before then, or it slips into the next month.

The bathroom tap still drips. It is the same kind of washer as the garden hose, so buying a small mixed pack would fix both. While at the hardware shop, a new filter for the cooker hood would be good, and the tall hooks for the shed, so that the bikes can hang on the wall instead of leaning against the lawnmower.

A few calls to make. The bank wants a signed form for the new account, which needs a stamp from the post office. The dentist moved the check up by a week, and the new time clashes with the team meeting, so that needs moving again. And the library sent a reminder about two books that are overdue, which are probably in the car.

Finally, a thought about the shed itself. It is full of things that nobody has touched in years: paint tins, old curtain rails, a broken chair and a box of cables. One clear Saturday would empty most of it. The paint can go to the tip on the first Sunday of the month, the rails and the chair to the charity shop, and the cables to the same recycling collection as the laptops.

That is everything for now. The order for the weekend is the cupboards, then the tomatoes and the hose, then the shed if there is time. Monday is the electrician, the summary sheet and the call to the garage. The rest can wait until the tiles are chosen, because most of the other decisions follow from that one.

One more thing about the kitchen. The new oven needs its own circuit, and the electrician wants to know the model before Friday so that he can bring the right cable. The model number is on the delivery note in the drawer by the front door. If the note has gone, the shop can send it again, but they only answer the phone in the mornings.

About the windows. The frames on the north side are swollen and stick every time it rains. The joiner said he could plane them down and repaint them for much less than new frames would cost. He is free in the second half of the month. It would be good to have that done before the autumn, when the rain comes in sideways off the hill.

The heating engineer sent a quote for the boiler service. It is a little higher than last year, but it now includes the radiator valves, which have been stiff since the spring. The earliest slot is a Thursday morning, and someone needs to be home from eight until noon. That might be the day to work from the kitchen table, if the kitchen still has a table by then.

A note for the weekend shopping. We are out of olive oil, rice and coffee, and the dishwasher tablets are nearly gone. The market on Saturday has the good bread, but it sells out by ten. The big supermarket is open late on Friday, which might be the better time for the heavy things, because the car park is empty after eight in the evening.

For the birthday at the end of the month, the restaurant can take a table for eight on the Saturday, or for ten if we move to the room at the back. They need the final number a week before, and a deposit when we confirm. Two people have not replied yet, so it is worth sending one short reminder on Monday and then booking for whoever has answered.

The bikes need some attention before the trip. The back tyre on the blue bike loses air over a few days, which usually means a slow puncture. The chain on the other bike is dry and noisy. Both are small jobs, and the shop on the corner does a quick service while you wait if you arrive before eleven. Otherwise it takes two days, and we need the bikes at the weekend.

At work, the new starter begins on the first Monday of next month. Their laptop has been ordered, but the desk by the window still has someone's boxes on it from the move. The welcome plan is mostly written. It needs a buddy for the first two weeks, a list of the systems they need access to, and a lunch on the first day, which should be booked somewhere close.

The training budget question will come up again at the meeting. Last year most of it went on one conference, and several people said they would rather have smaller courses spread across the year. A simple way to decide would be to ask everyone for one course they would choose, with its price, and then see how far the budget goes before anyone argues about it.

Last of all, the photos from the summer are still on the old phone. They should go onto the shared drive before the phone is wiped and sold. There are about two thousand of them, so it is a job for an evening with a cable rather than a slow upload over the air. After that, the phone can go back to its box with the charger, ready for whoever buys it.
